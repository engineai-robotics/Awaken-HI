Highlight

  • Awaken-HI uses a shared action expert for humanoid loco-manipulation to predict original human motion together with robot actions.
  • Awaken-Human aligns 50,000 hours of first-person stereo recordings with full-body human motion across diverse activities and settings.
  • With more human pretraining data, action prediction error falls along an empirical power law and real-robot success improves. Awaken-HI generalizes to new object–destination pairings and objects unseen during robot post-training.

Model

Awaken-HI uses a shared action expert so that original human motion directly trains the network that predicts T800 actions. During pretraining, three branches learn to predict human body and hand motion, retargeted T800 actions, and camera-frame wrist poses using flow matching. They share a state encoder and a diffusion transformer that cross-attends to the image and instruction features from Qwen3-VL. On the robot, the T800 action branch uses the head-camera RGB image and robot state to generate a sequence of 40-dimensional whole-body targets for the low-level controller.

Pretraining data

Awaken-Human pairs 50,000 hours of egocentric video with synchronized whole-body human motion. The curated dataset spans more than 100 skills and contains 17.02 million episodes, 5.41 billion pose frames, and 11.18 million valid language instructions. A PICO headset and five body-worn trackers record demonstrations as participants adapt how they walk, stand, and grasp to each setting. The annotation pipeline recovers 3D hand motion from video and adds subtask descriptions and stereo-derived depth.

Scaling human experience

We evaluate four nested pretraining subsets (5k, 12.5k, 25k, and 50k hours) on the same 100-hour held-out set. The scaling fits compare checkpoints at equivalent epochs. From the smallest subset to the full corpus, inference MSE on normalized T800 actions falls by about 11%, and validation flow-matching loss falls by 18.8%. Both metrics follow empirical power-law scaling with the amount of human pretraining data.

Real-world evaluation

Awaken-HI averages 93.75% success on individual T800 tasks, ahead of π₀.₅ and GR00T N1.7. Each method post-trains one multitask policy on the same 150 hours of teleoperation data and runs ten trials per task from matched starting configurations. Long-horizon trials succeed when the robot completes every stage in order without resets.

Conclusion

Awaken-HI uses one action expert to predict both original human motion and retargeted robot actions. Awaken-Human aligns 50,000 hours of first-person stereo recordings with whole-body motion. With more pretraining data, action prediction error on held-out examples falls along an empirical power law, and success on downstream tasks improves. After robot post-training, one policy averages 93.75% success on eight individual loco-manipulation tasks. The same policy generalizes to objects unseen during robot post-training and to new pairings of familiar objects and destinations. For more details, see the paper.
